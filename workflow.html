Crowbarr · subtitle timing service

How Crowbarr works, and where it sits

Bazarr already finds and downloads subtitles automatically, and even tries to sync them. Nothing checks whether the result is actually right. Crowbarr is that check: it listens to the audio, measures the subtitle against the dialogue it hears, and repairs the timing only when the audio proves it is wrong.

Where it sits

Crowbarr does not download subtitles and is not a player. It sits after Bazarr and before you watch.

Sonarr / RadarrImport the episode or film

How the verdict is reached

Timing is judged on when each caption starts. When it ends is a reading-time decision — subtitlers deliberately hold a line on screen after the speaker stops — and shifting a subtitle cannot change how long a line stays up. Only a caption that disappears before its line is finished counts as a fault.

Known gaps

Honest limits of the current build, not a roadmap.

  • Sampling stops at 85% of the runtime. The final stretch is extrapolated, and the re-check uses the same windows, so drift right at the end can be missed.
  • Crowbarr never tells Bazarr it disagreed. Bazarr can re-sync or upgrade the same subtitle later, unaware its previous result was judged wrong.
  • Bazarr's provider and score are ignored. The hook sends them; the decision does not use them.
  • Plex shows both English tracks identically. It only labels recognised flags like SDH or forced, so the corrected track cannot be named — you switch between them by trying one.
  • "Needs attention" is a dead end. Nothing automatically revisits a job the audit could not resolve.
